This is, as the title says, a comprehensive timeline of everything. No sources here, sorry. Go to the event the page is listed on. Citing takes too long.

## Pre 1900s

#### Pre-Colonization
- Two-spirit peoples
- Amazonian Queen Califia
#### 1840s
- 1849 - Gold Rush pulls in thousands of immigrants. [[Terms/Hanky code\|Hanky code]] begins to originate amongst miners

#### 1860s
- 1860- Whitman writes poem "Number 30" in the Calamus cluster of *Leaves of Grass*, later titled "A Promise to California" in the 1867 edition

#### 1870s
- 1879 - Time capsule buried in Washington Square

#### 1880s
- Queer rights movements begin in Germany

#### 1890s
- Getrude Stein & Alice B. Toklas grow up in the Bay Area
## Early 1900s

- 1906 - [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/The Black Cat Cafe\|The Black Cat Cafe]] opens for the first time as a vaudeville club
- 1921 - [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/The Black Cat Cafe\|The Black Cat Cafe]] closes for the first time due to suspicion of it being a front for prostitution
- 1922 Dec 12 - José Sarria is born
- 1929 - Finocchio's opens as the first gay bar post-prohibition
- 1930 May 22 - [[Cast/Main Figures/Harvey Milk\|Harvey Milk]] is born on Long Island
- 1933 - City's first two explicitly gay [[Terms/Bathhouses\|bathhouses]], Jack’s Turkish Baths and Third Street Baths, open
- 1933 December 5 - Prohibition repealed
- 1934 - Mona's, the nation's first lesbian bar, opens
- 1940s - [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/The Black Cat Cafe\|The Black Cat Cafe]] turns into a place for the Beat generation and bohemian folk
- 1940s - WWII "blue discharges" lead to thousands of gay soldiers staying in San Francisco
- 1948 - [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/The Black Cat Cafe\|The Black Cat Cafe]] liquor license suspended, they appeal
- 1950s - Homophile organizations begin to form in response to increased homophobia
- 1950 - Harry Hay and four men found the [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/Mattachine Society\|Mattachine Society]] in Los Angeles, forming chapters around the country
- 1951 - Supreme Court rules in favor of [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/The Black Cat Cafe\|The Black Cat Cafe]], landmark that homosexuals have a right to assemble socially 
- 1954 - Hal Call starts publishing *Mattachine Review* in San Francisco
- 1955 - Ginsberg reads his poem "Howl," sparking an obscenity hearing
- October 1955 - Founding of [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/Daughters of Bilitis\|Daughters of Bilitis]] (DOB) by four lesbian couples, led by Del Martin & Phyllis Lyon
- 1957 - Mona's closes
- 1959 - [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/Daughters of Bilitis\|DOB]] start holding "public discussion nights" to hear gay complaints
## 1960s
#### 1960

#### 1961
- Mattachine dissolves as a national organization, becomes local groups instead
- Guy Strait founds the League for Civil Education (LCE), the first organized attempt to build up a gay voting bloc. Distributed the city's first gay tabloid, *Citizen News*, free of charge in bars
- "Gayola" scandal occurs where SFPD & ABC officers took bribes from gay bar owners
- September 14 - Tay Bush Inn raid occurs, largest police raid in SF history
- November 7 - Jose Sarria runs for supervisor in response to Tay Bush raid. Although he doesn't win, establishes the potential power of the gay voting bloc 
#### 1962
- Formation of Tavern Guild, first gay business association
- The Tool Box, an S/M bar, opens
#### 1963
- Halloween - [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/The Black Cat Cafe\|The Black Cat Cafe]] loses its liquor license and tries to operate on a non-alcoholic menu
#### 1964
- The Big Glass, the city's first African American gay bar, opens
- February - [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/The Black Cat Cafe\|The Black Cat Cafe]] shuts down permanently, many [[Context/Gay Bars\|gay bars]] follow suit 
- June 26 - Life declares San Francisco "The Gay Capital of the World" and features The Tool Box & Jumpin' Frog
#### 1965
- Jan 1- New Year's Eve benefit organized by the Council on Religion and the Homosexual, an alliance of [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/Society for Individual Rights\|SIR]], [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/Mattachine Society\|Mattachine]], [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/Daughters of Bilitis\|DOB]], etc. working together with Glide Memorial Methodist Church. Police raid it.
- In response to outrage at police treatment of the benefit, the case goes to trial and leads to the addition of a police liaison to the gay community
- DOB & SIR worked to found the Citizens' Alert, a 24 hour hotline that responded to reports of police brutality
#### 1966
- April - SIR opens the first gay community center in the nation on 6th Street
- May 21 - "Rally to Protest Exclusion of Homosexuals from the Armed Forces"
- First national convention of lesbian and gay groups, formation of North American Conference of Homophile Organizations (NACHO)
- Compton's Cafeteria is raided by police, and one of the queens throws coffee in the officer's face 
#### 1967
- Police raid at [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/The Black Cat Tavern\|The Black Cat Tavern]]
- [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/Society for Individual Rights\|Society for Individual Rights]] reaches over 1,200 members, becoming the largest homophile organization in the country
#### 1968

#### 1969
- Spring 1969 - Committee for Homosexual Freedom (CHF) is the first gay liberation group in the Bay Area
- June - [[Context/Queer Culture in New York/Stonewall\|Stonewall]]
- Trans, lesbian, and S/M inclined queers begin to be isolated from the general movement and form their own communities/movements
- SIR helps [[Cast/Main Figures/Dianne Feinstein\|Dianne Feinstein]] get elected
## 1970s
#### 1970
- June 27 - 20-30 people march down Polk Street to City Hall as part of San Francisco's first gay rights march
#### 1971
- [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/Alice B. Toklas Memorial Democratic Club\|Alice B. Toklas Memorial Democratic Club]] founded by Jim Foster, leader of [[Context/Organizations of Relevance/Society for Individual Rights\|SIR]]'s political committee
#### 1972
- Senator George McGovern runs for president and is supported by the Toklas club. In one night, they gathered 1/3rd of the votes needed to get him on the Norther California ballot
#### 1973
- March 3 - Milk & his partner Scott Smith open a camera shop on Castro Street
- Milk has his first bid for office. Running as an openly-gay hippy, he alienated the moderate vote and had no endorsements from the leading gay political organizations of the time. Despite placing 10th out of 32 candidates, he got 17,000 votes.
#### 1975
- Consenting Adult Sex Bill is passed, decriminalizing homosexuality in California. Supporters include [[Cast/Main Figures/George Moscone\|George Moscone]]
- Gay Freedom Day Parade becomes the largest gay parade in the nation and the largest even in San Francisco, drawing over 80,000 attendees
#### 1976
- Armistead Maupin's queer weekly serial novel, *Tales of the City*, starts publishing
- [[Terms/Homophobia\|Homophobia]] was ramping up again
- June 27 - "Dykes on Bikes" tradition is born at San Francisco Pride
#### 1977
- World's first gay film festival takes place in San Francisco
- Anita Bryant leads her "Save the Children" campaign, and a Dade County gay rights ordinance is rolled back. San Franciscoans organize to raise money and boycott orange juice
- John Briggs announces [[Context/Legislation/Proposition 6\|Proposition 6]], a statewide initiative that would fire any gay or lesbian teachers in California public schools
#### 1978
- January 9 - Harvey Milk is sworn in to the Board of Supervisors, making him the first openly gay city official in California history
- June 25 - Gilbert Baker debuts the original pride flag at San Francisco Pride
- Proposition 6 fails to pass, with a 58% [NO] vote.
- November 18 - [[Events/Jonestown\|Jonestown]] massacre
- November 27 - Moscone & Milk are assassinated
#### 1979
- May 21 - Dan White is convicted of manslaughter, White Night riots begin
